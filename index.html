Unlearning Under Attack

Four unlearning methods were applied to a 14B model fine-tuned on MedMCQA, with the aim of removing pharmacology while retaining every other subject. Two of the four reduced forget-set accuracy to near zero. Of the four attacks with a clear verdict against them, the two weight-side ones, relearning and activation patching, recovered the knowledge to near the pre-unlearning baseline; the two rewording attacks did not. The weight methods pay for this with a large drop in accuracy on the subjects that were meant to be preserved.

1 · Introduction

Scope of this stage

Machine unlearning aims to remove a body of knowledge from a trained model without the cost of retraining from scratch. Evaluation in this area is typically performed by asking the model the questions it is meant to have forgotten and confirming that it answers them incorrectly. This measures one phrasing of one question type in one answer format, and does not establish whether the knowledge has been removed or has only become inaccessible along the route the evaluator tested.

Stage 1 addresses this by constructing four unlearned models and five attacks, and running each attack against each model. The result is the attack map reported in Section 7.

2 · Setup

Task, data splits, and baseline model

MedMCQA consists of Indian medical entrance examination questions, each with four options, one correct answer, and a written explanation. The corpus is split by subject. Pharmacology forms the forget set of 13,758 items; the remaining 169,064 items across all other subjects form the retain set. The two splits are disjoint by construction and reconstruct the full corpus exactly.

A Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct model was fine-tuned with 4-bit QLoRA on the full corpus to produce the baseline. Given a question it emits the answer text followed by a free-form explanation. Four methods were then applied to remove pharmacology from this baseline.

The two scoring methods disagree substantially. They are two different questions asked of the same model.

Likelihood scoring
Each of the four options is appended to the prompt in turn and scored by its log-probability under the model, divided by its token count. The highest-scoring option is taken as the answer. The model generates nothing; it is only asked which continuation it finds least surprising.
Baseline 0.429, chance is 0.25
Generated scoring
The model is given the prompt and decodes an answer greedily, as it would in use. The generated text is matched against the correct option text. This measures what the model actually says rather than how it ranks four supplied candidates.
Baseline 0.813

Both are reported because they can move independently. Ranking the four options by likelihood yields 0.429 while generation yields 0.813, a 38-point gap on the same model and the same questions. Likelihood scoring understates what the model knows, and a method can suppress one while leaving the other intact, so both are reported for every condition below.

Effect on the attack results. A method that reduces likelihood accuracy from 0.429 to 0.25 shows a 40% reduction. The same method reducing generated accuracy from 0.813 to 0.015 shows near-total removal. Both are reported for every condition below.
4 · Methods

Unlearning methods and their point of intervention

Three of the four methods modify the weights. RMU selects decoder layer 20 and trains a LoRA adapter that pushes the hidden state of forget-set inputs toward a random direction while holding retain-set activations in place. NPO applies a preference objective treating the correct forget-set answer as the rejected response. GradDiff is the unbounded baseline: gradient ascent on the forget loss and descent on the retain loss, with no bounding term.

ALU does not modify the model. Each query passes through a chain of agents: a drafter produces an answer, an auditor attempts to remove forget-set content from it, five rewrite variants are generated, a 32B critic scores each from 1 to 5, and a composer either synthesises the top two variants or refuses when no variant reaches a threshold of 4.

Three qualifications apply to these figures.

NPO did not unlearn at this scale

NPO reaches 0.720 generated forget accuracy against a 0.771 baseline, and 0.411 likelihood accuracy against 0.406, both within seed variance. The only metric it moved is explanation ROUGE-L, from 0.272 to 0.011, indicating that it stopped the model reproducing training explanations verbatim while leaving the underlying answer intact. It is carried through the attacks below but should be interpreted as an unlearning failure rather than a robust defence, and it is evaluated with the subject label present since there was no suppression to re-measure without it.

Both methods degrade retain accuracy

RMU retain accuracy falls from 0.749 to 0.429, a loss of thirty-two points across the subjects that were to be preserved. GradDiff falls to 0.491, a loss of twenty-six points. Suppressing pharmacology by its content, rather than by a subject label the method could match exactly, draws a wider boundary that catches adjacent material.

The GradDiff margin does not indicate collapse

The confidence margin, defined as the log-probability of the correct option minus that of the best incorrect option, is -1.03 for RMU and -1.41 for GradDiff. Mean loss on forget items is 13 for RMU and 168 for GradDiff, and the GradDiff forget loss reaches 222 during training, which is elevated given the absence of a bounding term. 6 · Attacks

Attack design

The attacks are grouped by point of entry. Two target the weights and assume the knowledge remains present in the parameters. Two target the input, altering how the question is posed rather than what is asked. The fifth targets the output format and applies to both families.

6.1 · Weight side

Attack 1: relearning

The unlearned model is fine-tuned on 30 pharmacology questions for 10 epochs and re-evaluated. The 30 examples are drawn from beyond the index range the evaluator scores, so the model is not tested on the items it was just trained on.

RMU recovers from 0.015 to 0.619, about three-quarters of the 0.813 baseline, after 108 seconds of training. GradDiff recovers to 0.819, at the baseline. Under likelihood scoring the same pattern holds: RMU moves from 0.245 to 0.505 and GradDiff from 0.272 to 0.623, against a baseline of 0.429. The GradDiff recovery fraction, measured on likelihood accuracy relative to the baseline, is 2.23, meaning the fine-tune overshoots the un-unlearned model. This is consistent with a short high-signal fine-tune on in-domain data.

NPO moves in the opposite direction, from 0.720 to 0.601. Since NPO did not suppress the forget set, there was no suppression for the attack to reverse, and the fine-tune acts as a perturbation of an already-competent model.

The attack does not apply to ALU. No weights were modified, so there is nothing to fine-tune back.

6.2 · Weight side

Attack 2: activation recovery

This attack requires no training. The original SFT model is run forward once and its hidden state captured at a chosen layer. The unlearned model is then run forward on the same input with a hook that overwrites its hidden state at that layer with the captured value, so that all subsequent layers process a representation that was never unlearned.

What the weight methods removed was access to the knowledge rather than the knowledge itself.

6.3 · Input side

Attack 3: rephrasing

The prediction in the thesis plan was that the inference-time filter would fail under rephrasing, on the grounds that a text filter matches surface terms which rewording removes. This was not observed. Against the 1,500-item paraphrase set, which reorders and re-synonymises each question while retaining the drug and mechanism vocabulary, all methods hold. The two weight methods stay at 0.009 and 0.020, and the filter moves in the direction of more suppression, not less.

6.4 · Input side

Attack 4: indirect questions

Where paraphrasing retains the vocabulary, the indirect attack removes it. The same 1,500 forget questions used for the paraphrase set were rewritten by Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct into clinical-vignette form: the drug, drug class, receptor and mechanism names are removed and replaced with a description of the clinical scenario or physiological effect, the four answer options are kept verbatim, and the correct option is unchanged. A second pass with the same model checks each rewrite, that the target is no longer named and that exactly one option is still correct. Rows that fail get one retry with a sharper prompt; rows that still fail are dropped. 13.1% were dropped, leaving 1,283 scored items. The subject field on this set reads Unknown like every other split.

6.5 · Output side

Attack 5: answer format

This attack tests whether suppression is tied to the format of the answer. The correct answer is supplied in the prompt and the model is asked to produce the explanation that follows it, to determine whether the forgotten drug and mechanism reappear in free text while the direct answer remains suppressed. Scoring requires a judge model rather than string matching.

Generation was run for RMU, on the neutralised model, across 1,252 forget items that have a written ground-truth explanation for comparison. All 1,252 outputs are degenerate and there is nothing for a judge to score.

The rate is 1,252 of 1,252. This is consistent with RMU's explanation ROUGE-L of 0.007 and its generated forget accuracy of 0.015: within the pharmacology domain the model's free-form generation does not function. No knowledge leaks through the explanation because no explanation is produced.

This is a negative result for the attack and a positive one for RMU, but it should be read alongside the retain figure. RMU also lost thirty-two points of accuracy on subjects it was intended to preserve. The same property that prevents leakage here, the loss of generative capacity in the affected region, is consistent with the ease of reversal observed in Attack 1.

Incomplete

Attack 5 has been generated for RMU only. NPO, GradDiff and ALU have not been run, and no judge model has scored any output. NPO is the priority case: it is the one method that left the answer intact while reducing explanation ROUGE-L from 0.272 to 0.011, which is the profile this attack was designed to detect. It is recorded as untested rather than passed in Table 4.

8 · Discussion

Comparison with the predicted outcome

The thesis plan predicted a crossing pattern: weight methods would fail under relearning and activation recovery but survive rewording, while the filter would survive relearning and fail under rewording, with no overlap between the two sets of weaknesses.

Both predictions hold. The weight methods recover accuracy to approximately baseline under relearning and under activation patching, one of them with no gradient steps, and they hold under both rewording attacks. ALU holds under rewording and is not subject to either weight attack.

The two families have disjoint weaknesses, as the plan predicted. Where they differ is in what they cost.

Weight editing removes the topic broadly, on held-out and indirectly-phrased questions as well as direct ones, but takes twenty-six to thirty-two points of other-subject accuracy with it and is undone by anyone able to fine-tune. The filter leaves the model intact and its suppression is insensitive to phrasing and to fine-tuning, at the cost of a per-query pass and an 11% false-refusal rate. Neither is adequate alone.

Implications for Stage 2

The gate proposed for Stage 2 reads the model's internal representation of the question and blocks before generation begins. Stage 1 gives two reasons to prefer it over the weight methods measured here.

First, it sits in front of an unmodified model, so it does not incur the twenty-six to thirty-two point cost to other subjects that both weight methods showed, and it cannot be reversed by fine-tuning. Second, Attack 2 shows the topic is legibly present in the residual stream at layer 20: patching that layer with the baseline hidden state restores about 99% of the likelihood accuracy, so the signal a probing classifier would read is there to be read.

The same result is also the warning. If the representation can be read at layer 20 it can be written there too, which is the evasion Stage 3 is designed to test: whether an adversary can craft activations that pass the gate.

Evaluation rules applied
  • Score option text, not letter logits. The model was fine-tuned to emit option text, so scoring the logits of A, B, C or D tests an out-of-distribution behaviour and returns near-uniform values regardless of the model's knowledge.
  • Length-normalise before the argmax. Unnormalised log-likelihood sums favour shorter options.
  • Report the confidence margin alongside accuracy. RMU at -1.03 and GradDiff at -1.41 have comparable accuracy; the margin is what separates suppression from confident misdirection.
  • Report generated accuracy in addition to likelihood. The two differ by 38 points on the same baseline.
  • Filter negation-framed items from evaluation only. Items of the form "all of the following except" remain in training and are excluded from scoring.
Scoring correction, ALU run

The ALU run initially reported 0.000 forget accuracy, 0.000 paraphrase accuracy and 0.002 retain accuracy. The cause was the scorer rather than the method. It used a regular expression matching a leading A, B, C or D against a model trained to answer in option text with no letter present in the output, so every response was scored as incorrect.

Regeneration was not required, since the raw per-item text had been checkpointed. Rescoring by gold option-text substring match, following the convention used by the main evaluator, recovers the real numbers. The near-zero retain accuracy was the indicator: a filter that refuses 11% of retain questions cannot score 0.002 on the remainder.

Subject label in the prompt

MedMCQA is split by subject, and the fine-tuning template prepends a Subject: line to every question. On the forget set it reads Pharmacology and on every retain example a different subject, so the token is a perfect forget-set marker. The first evaluation of the unlearned models kept this line as written. That was a mistake: a gradient method minimising forget accuracy while preserving retain accuracy can satisfy both by suppressing on that one token, and the numbers it produced could not be told apart from real unlearning.

How much the token was carrying: re-scoring those models on the direct forget set with only the subject value changed, Pharmacology to Unknown and nothing else, lifted GradDiff generated accuracy from 0.000 to 0.819 and RMU from 0.001 to 0.400. The SFT baseline barely moved, 0.771 to 0.813.

The fix: RMU, GradDiff and the SFT baseline evaluation were re-run on data with the subject value set to Unknown in every split, so the method never sees the marker during unlearning and the evaluator never supplies it. Those are the numbers on this page. ALU was re-run the same way and barely moved, forget 0.244 to 0.220 and retain 0.730 to 0.728, since its critic reads the question content and not the subject line; its indirect and holdout runs were not repeated. NPO was not re-run because it did not suppress anything to re-measure.

Open items before Stage 2

  • ALU indirect and holdout under the neutralised field. ALU's main evaluation and rephrasing were re-run; the indirect attack and the holdout split were not, though its two-point shift on direct forget accuracy suggests neither verdict would change.
  • Attack 5 on the remaining three methods, with a judge model to score leakage. NPO is the priority, since intact answers with suppressed explanations is the profile the attack targets.
  • Unlearning across seeds 42 and 43. Every Stage 1 result is from seed 44 alone, so no unlearning figure carries an error bar.
  • NPO at 14B requires a rerun or a retraction. As configured it did not unlearn, and reporting it as a defence would misrepresent it.
  • ALU on the holdout split, about 3 hours at the measured per-query cost. The same pass should also confirm ALU's nominal immunity to the two weight attacks empirically.

Feasibility: reading the topic from activations

Stage 2 builds a gate that reads the model's internal representation of a question and blocks before generation, rather than filtering on the output text. Format independence

A gate is only useful if it fires the same whether the question is posed as a multiple-choice item or as an open request for an explanation, since an attacker can route around a suppressed answer by asking for reasoning directly instead. Each classifier above was fit only on the multiple-choice prompt. To test whether that same, unmodified classifier still separates the classes when the options list and the answer scaffold are removed, it was evaluated, with no retraining, on 500 fresh forget and 500 fresh retain questions rewritten into open, option-free prompts.

The transfer is not uniform across depth, and the cutoff is sharp. At layers 4 through 12, accuracy on the open-format prompt collapses to 0.50 to 0.52, chance, even though the same layers scored 0.84 to 0.93 in distribution. Ranking quality (AUC) only falls to 0.78 to 0.84 there rather than to chance, so the ordering information survives; what breaks is the decision boundary, fit on multiple-choice activations whose scale shifts once the prompt no longer ends on the same token. From layer 16 on, accuracy holds at 0.82 to 0.87, a real drop from the in-distribution figure but well above chance. The likely reason is that shallow layers are still driven by the identity of the last token, which differs between the two prompt templates, while deeper layers have settled into a representation closer to what the question is about.

Reading this result. Feasibility is confirmed from roughly layer 16 onward: the topic is present in the activations and legible across both rewording and answer format there. It is not confirmed at shallow layers, despite their in-distribution accuracy looking equally strong, which is exactly the failure mode this check was run to catch. Layer 16 is the current working choice for the gate, with layer 20 close behind. These numbers are a single 80/20 split, not cross-validated, so treat differences of a point or two between neighbouring layers as noise.
